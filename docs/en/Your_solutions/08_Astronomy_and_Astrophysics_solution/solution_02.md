

### Part 1: Calculations and Comparison

To find out which is faster, we need to calculate the orbital speeds of both the International Space Station (ISS) and the Earth.

**1. Orbital Speed of the ISS around Earth**
The formula for the orbital velocity of an object in a circular orbit is:


$$v = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{r}}$$


Where:

* $G$ is the gravitational constant ($6.6743 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}^3 \text{kg}^{-1} \text{s}^{-2}$)
* $M$ is the mass of the central body (Earth's mass, $M_E \approx 5.97 \times 10^{24} \text{ kg}$)
* $r$ is the distance from the center of the central body to the orbiting object.

The Earth's radius ($R_E$) is approximately $6,371 \text{ km}$. The ISS altitude ($h$) is $400 \text{ km}$.


$$r = R_E + h = 6,371 \text{ km} + 400 \text{ km} = 6,771 \text{ km} = 6,771,000 \text{ m}$$

Plugging in the numbers:


$$v_{ISS} = \sqrt{\frac{(6.6743 \times 10^{-11}) \times (5.97 \times 10^{24})}{6,771,000}}$$

$$v_{ISS} \approx \sqrt{58,847,688} \approx 7,671 \text{ m/s}$$


**Speed of ISS = 7.67 km/s**

**2. Orbital Speed of Earth around the Sun**
For Earth, we can calculate the average orbital speed using the distance traveled in one orbit (circumference) divided by the time it takes (orbital period).


$$v = \frac{2 \pi d}{T}$$


Where:

* $d$ is the distance to the Sun ($150 \times 10^6 \text{ km} = 1.5 \times 10^{11} \text{ m}$)
* $T$ is the orbital period in seconds ($365.25 \text{ days} \times 24 \text{ hours/day} \times 3600 \text{ seconds/hour} \approx 31,557,600 \text{ s}$)

Plugging in the numbers:


$$v_{Earth} = \frac{2 \pi (1.5 \times 10^{11})}{31,557,600}$$

$$v_{Earth} \approx 29,865 \text{ m/s}$$


**Speed of Earth = 29.87 km/s**

**Conclusion:**
**Earth in its orbit around the Sun (~29.87 km/s) is almost 4 times faster than the ISS in its orbit around the Earth (~7.67 km/s).**
