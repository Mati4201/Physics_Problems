To find the speed at which a 1-meter ruler contracts to 0.5 meters, we use the formula for **length contraction** from Albert Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity:

$$L = L_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$

Where:

* **$L$** is the observed contracted length ($0.5$ meters)
* **$L_0$** is the proper length of the object at rest ($1$ meter)
* **$v$** is the velocity of the object
* **$c$** is the speed of light in a vacuum ($\approx 3 \times 10^8$ m/s)

**Step-by-step Calculation:**

1. Substitute the known values into the equation:

$$0.5 = 1 \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$


2. Square both sides to remove the square root:

$$(0.5)^2 = 1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}$$


$$0.25 = 1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}$$


3. Rearrange the equation to solve for the velocity term ($\frac{v^2}{c^2}$):

$$\frac{v^2}{c^2} = 1 - 0.25$$


$$\frac{v^2}{c^2} = 0.75$$


4. Take the square root of both sides to find $v$ in terms of $c$:

$$\frac{v}{c} = \sqrt{0.75}$$


$$\frac{v}{c} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866025$$


5. Solve for $v$:

$$v \approx 0.866c$$
