

### **1: The Logic**




**The Core Concept:**
Einstein’s Theory of Special Relativity tells us that the speed of light ($c$) is constant for everyone, no matter how fast they are moving. Because the speed of light cannot change, *space and time must change* to accommodate it.

* **Proper Time ($\Delta t_0$):** This is the time measured by an observer who is *at rest* relative to the event being measured. In our problem, the spaceship is at rest relative to its own TV. So, the spaceship's 1-hour episode is the Proper Time.
* **Dilated Time ($\Delta t$):** This is the time measured by an observer moving relative to the event. To the people on Earth, the spaceship is moving incredibly fast, so the Earth observers see the spaceship's clock running slower.

To the Earthling, 2 hours have passed. To the Astronaut, only 1 hour has passed. Moving clocks run slow!

---

### **2: The Mathematical Solution**



**variables :**

* $\Delta t = 2$ hours (Time passed on Earth)
* $\Delta t_0 = 1$ hour (Proper time passed on the spaceship)
* $c =$ the speed of light
* $v =$ the velocity of the spaceship (what we are solving for)

**2. The Equation:**
We use the time dilation formula, which relies on the Lorentz factor ($\gamma$):

$$\Delta t = \frac{\Delta t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$

**3. Solving for $v$:**
Plug in our known times:

$$2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$

Square both sides to get rid of the square root:

$$4 = \frac{1}{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$

Rearrange the equation to isolate the velocity terms:

$$1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2} = \frac{1}{4}$$

$$\frac{v^2}{c^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

Take the square root of both sides to find $v$:

$$v = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}c \approx 0.866c$$

**The Conclusion:** The spaceship must be traveling at roughly **86.6% the speed of light** for the Earthling to watch two episodes while the astronaut watches just one.
