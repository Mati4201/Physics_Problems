
$$\Delta x \Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$$

Since momentum is the product of mass and velocity ($p = mv$), and the mass of an electron is constant, the uncertainty in momentum is entirely due to the uncertainty in velocity ($\Delta p = m \Delta v$).

Substituting this into the equation gives:

$$\Delta x (m \Delta v) \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$$

We can rearrange this to solve for the minimum uncertainty in velocity ($\Delta v$):

$$\Delta v \ge \frac{\hbar}{2m \Delta x}$$

Now, let's identify the known values:

* **Reduced Planck constant ($\hbar$)**: $\approx 1.055 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$ (or $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}$)
* **Mass of an electron ($m$)**: $\approx 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$
* **Uncertainty in position ($\Delta x$)**: $0.1 \text{ nm} = 1.0 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m}$

Plug these values into the rearranged equation:

$$\Delta v \ge \frac{1.055 \times 10^{-34}}{2 \cdot (9.11 \times 10^{-31}) \cdot (1.0 \times 10^{-10})}$$

$$\Delta v \ge \frac{1.055 \times 10^{-34}}{1.822 \times 10^{-40}}$$

$$\Delta v \ge 5.79 \times 10^5 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:**
The minimum uncertainty in the velocity of the electron is approximately **$5.79 \times 10^5$ m/s** (or 579 km/s).

*Context:* This is a massive inherent uncertainty—it equates to about 0.2% the speed of light. Because an atom is such a tiny, confined space, the electron's velocity cannot be pinned down exactly, demonstrating why classical mechanics breaks down at the quantum level and why electrons exist in "probability clouds" rather than definitive planetary orbits.
